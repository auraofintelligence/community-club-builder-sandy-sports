Identity and aims

Set the public purpose first. Later rules should support these aims rather than pull the club in a different direction.

1 Club name

Suggested answer

Minjerribah Sandy Sports Club Inc.

Other options

  • Sandy Sports Club Inc.
  • North Stradbroke Island Sandy Sports Club Inc.
  • A broader name if the club will cover more than sand-based sport.

Use of Minjerribah or other cultural naming remains subject to appropriate permission and guidance.

Meeting decision

2 Club objects

Suggested answer

  1. Provide inclusive, safe and affordable sand sport, recreation and wellbeing activities.
  2. Create welcoming opportunities for people of different ages, genders, backgrounds and abilities.
  3. Support women, girls and balanced leadership in community sport.
  4. Support youth participation, family-friendly events, volunteering and practical skill development.
  5. Work respectfully with Traditional Owner bodies, clubs, schools, community groups, businesses, Council and sporting organisations.
  6. Use digital tools and human-reviewed AI assistance to reduce volunteer workload while protecting privacy, fairness and member control.
  7. Operate as a not-for-profit association for community benefit.

Other options

  • Keep the objects narrowly focused on sport and recreation.
  • Add screen culture, community events or local media as supporting activities.
  • Add environmental care, responsible tourism or broader island wellbeing as express objects.

Meeting decision and amendments

3 Relationship to sites and other projects

Suggested answer

The club is a member, volunteer, sport and wellbeing organisation. It does not claim ownership, control, approval or cultural authority over any proposed site or related project without a separate written agreement.

Other options

  • Name a confirmed home ground after a lawful agreement exists.
  • Remain a mobile club using approved venues and temporary event sites.

Meeting decision

Membership and voting

Keep one ballot per eligible member while making the value and calculation of each ballot unambiguous.

4 Membership classes

Suggested answer

  • Adult Island Resident or Island Ratepayer Member, with voting rights.
  • Adult Non-Resident Member, with voting rights.
  • Junior Member, without voting rights until adulthood.
  • Social or Casual Member, without voting rights.
  • Life Member, awarded by members at a general meeting and retaining the voting status of their resident or non-resident class.

Other options

  • Founder-Lifetime memberships similar to the Point Lookout Fishing Club structure.
  • One adult membership class with no residency distinction.
  • Family, supporter, organisational or honorary classes with clearly limited rights.

Meeting decision

5 Voting power

Suggested answer

Each eligible member may cast one ballot. Island Resident and Island Ratepayer Members collectively hold 70% of the voting power. Non-Resident Members collectively hold 30%. Within each group, its voting power is divided equally among the valid ballots cast by that group on the question.

If votes are tied after weighting, the motion fails. The chairperson has no additional casting ballot.

Other options

  • Keep 70/30 weighting but divide power across all eligible members, including members who do not vote.
  • Use equal voting power for routine matters and 70/30 weighting only for major governance, site or constitutional decisions.
  • Use one member, one ballot and equal value for every question.
  • Allow a chairperson's casting ballot and state exactly how it is weighted.

Meeting decision and calculation method

6 Fees, admission and membership limits

Suggested answer

Membership numbers are unlimited unless safety or venue capacity requires a temporary intake limit. Members at a general meeting set fee categories. The committee sets payment dates, concessions and family discounts. Applications receive a fair committee decision and written notice.

Other options

  • Record fixed fees in the constitution.
  • Let the committee set all fees through a published annual schedule.
  • Cap particular membership classes.

Meeting decision

7 Suspension, termination and grievances

Suggested answer

Keep written notice, a fair opportunity to respond, reasons for the decision and a right of appeal. Include the current Queensland model-rule grievance and mediation process as the starting procedure for disputes under the rules.

Other options

  • Add an informal facilitated conversation before formal mediation.
  • Use an independent conduct panel for serious safety or member-protection matters.
  • Set a maximum suspension period and separate urgent temporary safety measures from final discipline.

Meeting decision

Committee and meetings

Share authority, preserve local accountability and make every critical role transferable.

8 Committee structure

Suggested answer

Elect a president or chair, deputy chair, secretary, treasurer and up to five ordinary committee members each year. Allocate participation, safety and wellbeing, events, partnerships and digital administration responsibilities after the election.

Every critical system must have a lead, a backup and committee-visible access.

Other options

  • Use co-chairs instead of a president and deputy.
  • Elect portfolio positions directly.
  • Set a smaller committee and appoint non-voting working groups.

Meeting decision

9 Committee eligibility and local leadership

Suggested answer

The secretary must meet Queensland's legal eligibility requirements and be an Island Resident. A majority of committee members must be Island Resident or Island Ratepayer Members. The club actively encourages women, young adults and people with different abilities and backgrounds into leadership.

Other options

  • Require only the chair and secretary to be Island Residents.
  • Use the legal minimum for residency without an extra island requirement.
  • Set leadership balance as policy rather than a binding constitutional rule.

Meeting decision

10 Meeting access, notice and proxies

Suggested answer

Allow in-person, hybrid and online participation where members can hear and take part as discussions happen. Give at least 14 days notice of general meetings. Do not use proxies because eligible members can participate remotely.

Other options

  • Permit written proxies using the model-rule form.
  • Permit absentee electronic ballots for stated resolutions.
  • Require physical attendance for specified major decisions.

Meeting decision

11 Conflicts, payments and major commitments

Suggested answer

Committee members declare personal, family, business, sponsor and property interests before discussion. The declaration and management action are recorded in the minutes. Reasonable payment for genuine work is allowed when properly approved and disclosed.

Financial commitments over $5,000 require approval by a three-quarter majority of weighted votes at a general meeting.

Other options

  • Use a different fixed threshold such as $2,000 or $10,000.
  • Set the threshold annually through a member-approved finance policy.
  • Require member approval for borrowing, leases or guarantees regardless of value.

Meeting decision

AI-native rules

AI can carry administrative weight, but legal authority and responsibility stay with people.

12 Authority of AI systems

Suggested answer

AI systems may assist the association but cannot be a member, office-holder, signatory, decision-maker or holder of delegated legal authority. An identified person must review every AI-assisted publication, lodgement, payment instruction, membership action, safety decision and official record.

Other options

  • Keep this principle in the constitution and place the detailed workflow in an AI use policy.
  • Require two-person review for higher-risk AI-assisted work.
  • Prohibit AI from particular areas such as discipline, child safety or financial approvals.

Meeting decision

13 Approved AI uses

Suggested answer

AI may help prepare agendas, draft minutes, summaries, reminders, member communications, rosters, grant drafts, risk prompts, policy drafts, public information and handover records using information approved for that purpose.

Other options

  • Approve named tools and uses in a committee-controlled register.
  • Allow only local or association-controlled systems for sensitive information.
  • Limit the first year to low-risk drafting and administration.

Meeting decision

14 Privacy, records and member access

Suggested answer

Personal, health, child-safety, financial, cultural and complaint information must not be placed into an AI system unless the committee has approved the system, purpose, access, retention and human reviewer. Members must have a practical non-digital way to participate.

Official records must remain exportable, backed up and accessible to at least two authorised people. AI-generated drafts do not become records until a responsible person accepts them.

Other options

  • Prohibit all sensitive information from third-party AI tools.
  • Require express member consent for specified AI-assisted processes.
  • Set deletion and retention periods in a separate privacy policy.

Meeting decision

15 Transparency, errors and the stop switch

Suggested answer

Material AI assistance is disclosed where it affects an official decision or public document. Members can request human review of an AI-assisted outcome. The committee keeps a simple incident record and may suspend an AI system immediately if it creates privacy, safety, fairness, security or reliability concerns.

Other options

  • Publish an annual AI use and incident summary.
  • Require member approval before introducing high-impact automated systems.
  • Appoint a digital administration and privacy officer.

Meeting decision

Money, records and winding up

Choose the practical settings that complete the constitution and keep the club's assets visible.

16 Banking and record custody

Suggested answer

Keep the association's funds in its own account. Committee expenditure must be approved or ratified and recorded. No single person has sole control of banking, passwords, member data, grants, equipment records or public accounts. At least two authorised people hold recoverable access.

Other options

  • Require two approvals for every electronic payment.
  • Use approval bands for routine, significant and major expenditure.
  • Require a monthly treasurer report and quarterly independent check.

Meeting decision

17 Financial year and surplus assets

Suggested answer

The financial year ends on 30 June. If the association winds up, surplus assets go to another not-for-profit entity with similar objects and rules preventing distribution to members. Members choose the recipient in accordance with the law.

Other options

  • Use a calendar-year financial year ending 31 December.
  • Name a preferred eligible recipient and provide a fallback selection process.
  • Add deductible gift recipient winding-up clauses if that endorsement will be sought.

Adoption and next actions

Do not treat suggestions on this page as adopted rules. Record each decision, prepare a complete draft, circulate it, check it against current official requirements and then put the final wording to the required member resolution.
