The committee is the human proof.

Agents can prepare the website, drive, forms, watchlists, grant pre-work and ecosystem map. The four officers carry the human part: playing, listening, showing up, choosing and holding public authority.

Four seats Selection spectrum Care boundary Human authority

Pick for trust in motion, not admin theatre.

The tools carry the scaffolding. The committee becomes the credible human layer that uses the tools, makes choices, corrects course and stays visible in the community.

President

Trusted community anchor

Could be a well-liked mother, teacher, health worker, respected organiser or calm sport/community connector.

Carries: purpose, trust, chairing, public voice and boundary discipline.

Watch for: status chasing, solo promises, political games or turning the club into one person's platform.

Vice President

Active program builder

Could be a coach, teacher, youth worker, beach person, fitness person or practical organiser who actually wants to play.

Carries: sessions, volunteers, invitations, event energy and the rhythm of learning by doing.

Watch for: chaos, cliques, overpromising or confusing popularity with readiness.

Secretary

Systems-native truth keeper

Could be a young adult at uni, an early-career organiser, a teacher-adjacent person or someone calm with digital tools.

Carries: minutes, decisions, source trails, member records and the difference between actuals, hypotheses and simulations.

Watch for: treating AI drafts as decisions, burying records or becoming the unpaid admin sink.

Treasurer

Clear-eyed money adult

Could be a small-business person, bookkeeper, finance-capable parent, retired operator or honest practical elder.

Carries: budgets, approvals, receipts, grant evidence, quotes, risk and the courage to say not yet.

Watch for: fear-based blocking, loose spending, hidden interests or pressure to make unfunded promises.

These are functions, not token seats.

A mother or family anchor can change who feels safe to try. A school-connected person can help youth pathways, as long as school authority and club authority stay distinct. A young adult can carry digital fluency, media sense and future culture, and is not cheap admin labour.

The filter is a spectrum, not a purity test.

These are signals to discuss openly. Nobody has to be perfect. The question is whether the group can trust the person under pressure, in public, around money, around conflict and around young people.

Well liked not people-pleasing

They can be warm without saying yes to every loud person.

Honest not rigid

They tell the truth and can still update when the facts change.

Energetic not chaotic

They move things forward without turning every idea into an emergency.

Healthy enough not performative

They model the culture: movement, care, rest, boundaries and respect for the body.

Decisive and adaptable

They can choose a next step without pretending the first answer is sacred.

Respected across pockets

They are trusted by more than one social circle, age group or family network.

Low agenda no capture

They are not chasing status, control, contracts, access or revenge.

Visible and willing

They will play, show up, listen, help, correct and be seen doing the work.

Community care is governance, not vibes.

A club that wants to be a meeting place protects the way people meet. The human team sets the standard for behaviour, conflict, youth safety, care and wellbeing without pretending to be counsellors, police or doctors.

Conduct

No bully lane

Intimidation, humiliation, threats, coercion and social punishment do not get quiet control. Patterns are recorded, concerns are heard, and fair process protects the room.

Care

Suicide-aware, not saviour mode

The club can reduce isolation, notice distress and keep support numbers visible. If life is in danger, call 000. For crisis support: Lifeline 13 11 14, Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800, or 13YARN 13 92 76.

Tone

Uplifting, not manic

Hope is welcome. Forced positivity is not. The club can be optimistic while staying honest about grief, pressure, injury, family stress and the limits of volunteer capacity.

Youth

Visible youth safety

Junior activity works best with clear permission, visible supervision, clean pickup patterns, no private backchannels and records that protect young people and volunteers.

Competition

Win, lose, participate

Sport is competitive by design. The culture can honour effort, skill, tactics and winning while teaching people to lose without shame and participate without being treated as lesser.

Conflict

Disagreement without humiliation

People can disagree without being mocked, cornered or exiled. Feedback, complaints, conflicts of interest and behaviour breaches belong in different lanes.

Repair

Repair beats image control

When harm happens, the useful move is to support the affected person, name the behaviour, record the decision and change the conditions that let it happen.

Care is the balancing centre.

Money, media, awareness, influence, partners and competitive ambition are real components, not idols and not enemies. Over the life of the club, the human team and community keep balancing them against the deeper aim: helping people enjoy, connect, play, learn, share, teach, strengthen, heal, recover and feel less alone or helpless. Each opportunity, match, loss, win or urgent situation asks for its own response.

Agents prepare options. Humans discern.

The operating system can remove the dull load, but it cannot replace relationship, taste, courage, care or legal authority. The committee is there to walk the walk, not to become clerks for a machine.

Agents can prepare

Web pages, forms, folders, grant drafts, tender scans, meeting packs, media options, risk notes, source trails and missing-field checks.

Humans hold the choice

Priorities, tone, spending, public claims, partner approaches, conflict responses, cultural boundaries and what the club is ready to carry.

The committee carries the culture

Sport, health, hospitality, honesty, mixed-gender leadership, responsible flexibility and real community presence.
