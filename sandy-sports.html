Sandy Sports is a missing-link example, not a claimed facility.

There are no existing sand courts to inherit. The first useful move is a clean, portable community entity that can test low-pressure sand activity while any purpose-built Dunwich facility remains a future horizon.

Place roles Program mix Year-one proof

A distributed model keeps options open.

Amity was an early concept lane, beaches can support temporary trials, and Ballow Road is still uncertain paddock/context rather than a confirmed club asset. None of that should be confused with current approval, ownership or built infrastructure.

The club remains the people layer. Dunwich is the longer facility horizon if the right land, permission, partnership and community support come together.

Start with activities that are easy to enter and easy to stop.

Beach volleyball, beach cricket, roundnet, ultimate, family sand games, women's sessions, youth clinics and gentle wellbeing sessions can all be trialled before the club asks for a permanent footprint.

Year one success is quieter than a festival poster.

A clean committee, visible women in leadership, child-safety readiness if juniors are involved, three safe small activities, a working volunteer system and an honest evidence log are already meaningful.
