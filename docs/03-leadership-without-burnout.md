# Leadership Without Burnout

The club should be designed so one missing person does not stop the whole thing.

## The No Lone Hero Rule

Every important task needs:

- lead
- backup
- checklist
- shared folder
- next review date

If a task cannot meet those five conditions, it is too fragile.

## Suggested Committee Shape

Minimum legal committee thinking is not enough for a healthy club. A healthier shape:

| Role | Guards | Backup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chair or co-chair | Purpose, meetings, fair process | Deputy chair |
| Secretary | Minutes, member register, official notices | Assistant secretary |
| Treasurer | Money, payments, budget, financial records | Assistant treasurer |
| Safety and wellbeing lead | Risk, incidents, child safety, inclusion | Events lead |
| Volunteer and membership lead | Onboarding, rosters, recognition | Digital admin lead |
| Digital admin lead | Forms, folders, automations, AI prompts | Secretary |
| Events and sport lead | Program, coaches, gear, venue needs | Safety lead |

## Female-led And Mixed Gender By Design

Do not wait until the club "gets big" to fix culture.

Practical design choices:

- invite women to shape the founding purpose before roles are allocated
- avoid all-male founding committees
- use co-chairing if it helps balance confidence, time and experience
- schedule meetings at times that do not quietly exclude carers
- do not make women carry all wellbeing and admin work
- train younger women and girls as future coaches, scorers, media helpers and committee observers
- actively ask what would make the club feel safer and easier to join

Australian Sports Commission guidance supports promoting women into decision-making roles, building a pipeline for women contenders, providing training and developing gender-inclusive policies.

ASC women and girls guidance: <https://www.ausport.gov.au/integrity_in_sport/inclusive-sport/understanding-our-diverse-audiences/women-and-girls>

## Wrong Reasons Filter

Before someone joins the committee, ask:

1. What are you here to give?
2. What are you hoping to get?
3. Can you accept committee decisions you do not personally win?
4. Do you have any business, property, sponsor, family or political interest connected to the club?
5. Which boring job are you willing to help with for 3 months?

Good people can still have interests. The problem is hidden interests.

## Meeting Rhythm

Keep meetings small and predictable:

- monthly committee meeting
- quarterly open community huddle
- annual general meeting
- 15-minute post-event debrief after each activation

Every meeting should answer:

- What changed?
- What needs a decision?
- Who is doing the next small thing?
- What can AI draft or summarise so people are not stuck at midnight doing admin?

## Burnout Warning Signs

Watch for:

- one person replying to every message
- one person holding every password
- one person writing every grant
- meetings becoming complaint sessions
- people volunteering ideas but not labour
- social media moving faster than governance
- private messages replacing proper records

When this appears, stop adding projects. Fix the load.
