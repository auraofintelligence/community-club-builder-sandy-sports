# Legitimate Queensland Club Process

This is a plain-English pathway for a Queensland community sport or recreation club. Check the official links before lodging anything.

## 0. Decide Whether You Need Incorporation

An unincorporated group can meet, talk and test interest. But if the group wants to hold money, sign agreements, apply for grants, insure activities, hire venues or own equipment, incorporation is usually the cleaner path.

Incorporation means the club becomes its own legal body. Like a bucket: the club bucket holds the rules, money, records and responsibilities instead of one person holding them in their personal life.

Official starting point: <https://www.qld.gov.au/community/fair-trading/associations-charities-and-non-for-profits/incorporated-associations/set-up-an-incorporated-association>

## 1. Build The Founding Group

Before incorporation in Queensland, check the current requirement that the group must:

- have at least 7 members
- be not-for-profit
- have a physical Queensland address
- hold a general meeting to set up the association and adopt rules

Do not rush this. Seven names on paper is not the same as seven people who will share the load.

## 2. Choose A Name

Queensland guidance says the incorporated name must end with `Incorporated` or `Inc.` and cannot be too similar to another association, business, cooperative or company.

For the worked example, possible placeholder names are:

- Sandy Sports Club Inc.
- Minjerribah Sandy Sports Club Inc.
- Straddie Sand Sports Club Inc.

Do not imply approval, endorsement, affiliation with a Traditional Owner body, a sporting body, Council, the Olympic movement or a venue unless that relationship exists in writing.

## 3. Adopt Rules

The rules are the club's constitution.

Queensland lets an association use the Office of Fair Trading model rules or create its own rules. The model rules are the safest starting point for a new group. If the club changes the model rules, understand what is changing.

Official rules guidance: <https://www.qld.gov.au/community/fair-trading/associations-charities-and-non-for-profits/incorporated-associations/running-an-incorporated-association/rules-for-associations>

## 4. Elect A Management Committee

Queensland guidance says there must be a minimum of 3 committee members, such as president, treasurer and secretary.

For this club, 3 is legal minimum thinking. It is not healthy operating design. A better starting structure is:

- Chair or co-chair
- Secretary
- Treasurer
- Safety and wellbeing lead
- Volunteer and membership lead
- Digital admin lead
- Events and sport lead

Keep office-bearer duties small enough that a normal person with a life can do them.

Committee duties guidance: <https://www.qld.gov.au/community/fair-trading/associations-charities-and-non-for-profits/incorporated-associations/running-an-incorporated-association/incorporated-association-management-committee/what-committee-does>

## 5. Lodge The Incorporation Application

Queensland guidance says applications usually ask for the association name, committee details, rules, objects, and land information if the association owns or leases land. Fees and forms can change.

Forms and fees: <https://www.qld.gov.au/community/fair-trading/associations-charities-and-non-for-profits/incorporated-associations/incorporated-associations-forms-and-fees>

## 6. Set Up The Basic Compliance Stack

Once incorporated, set up:

- club email controlled by the committee, not one private person
- shared drive with folders for minutes, finances, policies, grants and events
- member register
- asset register
- meeting minutes template
- bank account with two-person approval
- public liability insurance check
- child safety and blue card process if children are involved
- annual return and AGM reminders
- ATO and ABN check

ATO NFP self-review return: <https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/not-for-profit-organisations/statements-and-returns/nfp-self-review-return-reporting-requirement>

ATO sporting organisations: <https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/not-for-profit-organisations/your-organisation/does-your-not-for-profit-need-to-pay-income-tax/types-of-income-tax-exempt-organisations/sporting-organisations>

## 7. Blue Cards And Child Safety

If the club works with children as part of sport or active recreation, Queensland blue card rules may apply to employees, volunteers and some executive members.

Sport and active recreation blue card guidance: <https://www.qld.gov.au/jobs/blue-card/required/individuals-businesses/sport-active-recreation>

When a blue card is not needed: <https://www.qld.gov.au/jobs/blue-card/required/when-you-dont-need-a-blue-card>

Treat child safety as a design rule, not paperwork. If juniors are involved, build the club so no adult is left alone to improvise.

## 8. Funding Pathways

Do not chase grants before the club can prove basic trust.

Early evidence to collect:

- member numbers
- volunteer hours
- attendance
- photos with consent
- incident-free events
- partnerships
- feedback
- accessibility improvements
- youth and women/girls participation

Useful public funding starting points:

- Redland City Council grants and sponsorship: <https://www.redland.qld.gov.au/Grants-and-sponsorship>
- Queensland sport and recreation funding: <https://www.qld.gov.au/recreation/activities/funding-and-grants>
- Active Clubs: <https://www.sport.qld.gov.au/sport/funding-programs/active-clubs>
- Games On legacy funding announcements: <https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/104414>

## 9. Site, Cultural And Land Permissions

Incorporation does not give the club the right to use a site.

For a Minjerribah sand sports example, the club would still rely on proper site permissions, Council conversations, insurance, cultural engagement and event approvals. Do that slowly and respectfully.

Simple rule: club first, trust next, place after that.
