# Marketing And Community Trust

Marketing works best as an invitation, not a sales funnel.

## The First Promise

The first public promise is:

> Come and help shape a welcoming, mixed-gender, family-friendly sand sports club that makes it easier for people to meet, move and belong.

Do not promise:

- permanent sites
- approvals
- funding
- cultural endorsement
- Olympic pathways
- celebrity patrons
- big festivals

Those may come later. Early overclaiming breaks trust.

## The Local Rhythm

Use a simple rhythm:

- Monday: what is happening this week
- Wednesday: one volunteer, member or local story
- Friday: weekend reminder
- After events: thank-you, attendance, photos with consent, next step

## Channels

Start with:

- simple public webpage or GitHub Pages site
- Facebook community post
- Instagram
- printed A6 flyer with QR code
- local noticeboards
- partner club newsletters
- school and family channels where appropriate
- ferry, cafe, pub and shop counters only after permission

## Story Rules

Every post works best when it does one job:

- invite
- thank
- explain
- report back
- ask for help

Avoid posting when angry. Avoid vague drama. Avoid "big future" language before the group has done the small present work.

## Community Trust Assets

Keep these public or easy to request:

- purpose statement
- founding meeting date
- committee list after election
- contact email
- code of conduct
- child safety summary
- volunteer pathway
- meeting summaries
- grant evidence reports

Trust grows when people can see the club's operating shape.

## Marketing Evidence For Grants

Record:

- post links
- flyer locations
- attendance counts
- volunteer hours
- local business support
- feedback quotes with permission
- photos with media consent
- accessibility notes
- gender and age participation patterns where collected respectfully
